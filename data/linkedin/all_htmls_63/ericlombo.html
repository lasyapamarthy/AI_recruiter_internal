Carnegie Mellon scientists just made it possible — with no cameras involved. Privacy gamechanger? Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University have developed a groundbreaking way to detect human movement through walls using everyday WiFi routers. By leveraging artificial intelligence, the team converted WiFi signals into a tool that can map the shape and motion of human bodies, even without direct visual access. Their system uses a neural network and DensePose — an AI model originally developed by Facebook — to interpret how WiFi signals bounce off moving people, effectively reconstructing their posture in real time. The setup is surprisingly affordable, using three standard routers and receivers, making this potentially accessible for average households. The implications are massive: from elderly care monitoring to home security, this tech could provide non-intrusive surveillance without cameras. However, concerns loom large around privacy. While the system may offer a level of anonymity traditional cameras lack, the ability to "see" through walls still raises ethical questions, especially regarding how “suspicious behavior” is defined and used. The interview started with basic technical questions, which the candidate answered well. Then, my team lead gave him a coding task: 👉 "Write a function that removes duplicate values from a nested array while preserving its structure (PHP)." The candidate struggled and couldn’t solve it. So, my lead gave him another task: 👉 "Write a function that groups an array of associative arrays based on a specific key." Again, he couldn’t solve it. Here,I decided to step in. Since I was working on WooCommerce project, I gave him this challenge: 💡 "Implement a functionality where a logged-in user gets a 15% discount if they place two orders in a single day." We allowed him to use Google, ChatGPT, or any other resources. To our surprise, within 1.5 hour, he understood WooCommerce functionality, analyzed the existing code, and successfully implemented the feature. ✨ We ended up hiring him! ✨ Key Takeaway Many traditional interview techniques overemphasize logical puzzles and syntax-heavy questions, yet modern developers approach problem-solving differently. Rather than memorizing solutions, they adapt, learn, and implement on the spot using the tools and resources available to them. 💡 Instead of testing how well candidates remember syntax, we should focus on how effectively they apply problem-solving skills to practical challenges.
