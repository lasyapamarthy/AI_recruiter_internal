Records are an amazing feature in C# that offer immutable data, value-based equality, and simplified code. They’re great for scenarios where data integrity is crucial, like in order processing systems. For example, in an e-commerce platform, a record can represent an Order DTO with properties like OrderId, CustomerName, and TotalAmount. Once the order is created, it cannot be modified, ensuring consistency across the system. Why use records in this case? 👉 Immutability: The data once created is unchangeable, ensuring the order's integrity throughout its lifecycle. 👉 Value-Based Equality: Two records with the same OrderId, CustomerName, and TotalAmount will be considered equal based on their values, not their memory location. Benefits in an Enterprise Context: 👉 Thread-Safety: Their immutability makes them naturally thread-safe critical in environments with high concurrency like in web services. 👉 Data Integrity: By ensuring data cannot be altered, records help preserve consistency and integrity across the entire application (e.g., UI, services, and database).
