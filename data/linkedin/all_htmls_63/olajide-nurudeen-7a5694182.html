Let’s break down the five key families (1xx-5xx) and what they really mean: 🔹 Information (1xx) These preliminary responses let you know your request is being processed. For instance, a 100 Continue means, “I got your request, now send me more.” 🔹 Success (2xx) The green lights of HTTP. A 200 OK means everything worked as expected. A 201 Created confirms that new resource you requested is now live! 🔹 Redirection (3xx) Navigation signals. A 301 or 302 redirects you to a new location, while 304 Not Modified boosts caching efficiency. 🔹 Client Errors (4xx) When things go wrong on our end. A 404 Not Found means the resource is missing, and a 403 Forbidden is a permissions issue—not a failure. 🔹 Server Errors (5xx) These are internal issues. A 500 Server Error indicates something broke on our side, and a 502 Bad Gateway suggests upstream trouble. Immediate action is needed! Understanding these codes isn’t just about coding—it’s about speaking the language of web systems. They’re your go-to diagnostic tool, your monitoring foundation, and the key to building resilient services. 💬 Which HTTP codes do you encounter most in your work?
