To even get started, I had to dive into two completely new technologies: Rust and Anchor. That’s when things got real. Rust, to me, feels like if TypeScript had a child with C++… while on steroids. Where do you even begin? Macros, attributes, ownership, memory management—it’s like learning how the computer actually thinks. I’ve been deep in The Rust Book like I’m cramming for finals, trying to wrap my head around stack vs. heap memory, borrowing rules (multiple immutable references vs. a single mutable one), and all the quirks that make Rust both powerful and intimidating. Then comes Anchor , the framework that’s supposed to make Solana development easier. If this is the easy version, I can’t even imagine what it was like before. And then there’s Solana itself, which is nothing like Ethereum. One major difference: on Solana, every piece of state is stored on-chain as an account with its own address. It’s a completely different architecture and mental model that takes some serious adjustment. The learning process has been fun—but also frustrating. Tutorials from just last year are already outdated or incompatible with current packages. Most aren’t beginner-friendly either, even if you’ve worked with blockchain before. What’s keeping me afloat is doing a lot of extra research—and leaning on my past experience with web3. To be honest, I’m still figuring out where it all begins and ends. But that’s part of the process. It’s challenging, it’s humbling, and it’s definitely exciting. If you’ve been down this path ,or are currently on it , I’d love to hear your thoughts or tips.
