As a product manager, I'm always on the lookout for ways to improve my product. Recently, I learned some really clever tactics that Duolingo uses to keep its users coming back for more, and I wanted to share them with you. 📅 Contextual Messages: If a student is planning a trip abroad 🛫, Duolingo triggers their emotions, situations, and frustrations 💬 with external triggers like Emails, offering the opportunity to learn the local language of their destination 🔥 Reduced Decision-Making Friction: Duolingo presents users with clear call-to-action shortcuts and limits the number of options available to them, making it easier for users to decide what to do next and reducing frustration 🤯 ✌🏼 Easy Flow for Disengaged Users: Duolingo helps users who returned after a long pause get back into the flow of learning with an easier, more enjoyable experience. This could include things like lower difficulty levels, incentives for returning 🏆, and highlighting micro signs of progress 📈 to keep users motivated and engaged. 🎁 Bigger Incentives for Returning: Duolingo rewards users with Gems 💎 for completing Daily goals after returning from a long pause, making the app more enjoyable and rewarding; Encouraging users to come back and engage more frequently 🎯 Gamifying the learning: Duolingo gamifies the learning experience with features like streaks and progress bars 🔥, reinforcing the Zeigarnik Effect (the tendency to remember uncompleted tasks better) to motivate users to complete their daily tasks 💪🏼 More streaks mean more engagement. 📈 Anticipation of Returns: Duolingo uses the Sunk-Cost Effect theory, to encourage users to invest a portion of their rewards 🏆 in anticipation of future benefits. This has been shown to increase 7-day Retention by 14% 🤩 [Source: Duolingo's blog] ⏰ Reminders: Duolingo sends notifications 📢 to remind users to complete their daily missions after committing to a streak 🔥 These reminders help keep users on track and motivated to continue learning. 📵 Filtering Notifications: If a user doesn't open the Duolingo app after several notifications, the app will filter out some of the notifications to respect the user's attention. I hope these examples give you some ideas for how you can use psychology to improve user retention and engagement in your own products.
