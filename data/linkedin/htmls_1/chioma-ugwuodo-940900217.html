Artificial intelligence has fundamentally transformed business decision-making processes, particularly in domains requiring the evaluation of complex information at scale \citep{davenport2018artificial}. Machine learning and natural language processing technologies have advanced significantly in recent years, enabling varying levels of automation of tasks previously thought to require human judgment \citep{brynjolfsson2017can,agrawal2022power, dell2025cybernetic}. Organizations searching for more efficient solutions or facing resource constraints increasingly deploy AI systems to augment human decision making, with applications spanning almost all areas including financial services (Cao et al., 2021), customer service (Fotheringham and Wiles, 2023), and human resource management (Tambe et al., 2019). This technological evolution has generated considerable academic and practitioner interest in understanding how AI systems influence decision outcomes and overall performance (von Krogh, 2018). Nevertheless, diffusion is uneven: recent survey evidence indicates that AI deployment is heavily skewed toward large, fast-growing firms with the requisite data and analytic talent \citep{BrynjolfssonAIAdoption}. Algorithm-averse behaviours by managers further moderate realised gains \citep{dietvorst2015algorithm, dietvorst2018overcoming}.
