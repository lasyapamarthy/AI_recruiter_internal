I’d forget big tech. Focus on global startups and solo founders. Big companies have long pipelines and ATS filters. But small teams are looking for hustlers, not just degrees. I’d target founders on LinkedIn and Twitter who are actively building. These people need help yesterday. 2. I’d package myself I’d position myself as someone who can wear 3 hats: support, content, and admin. Why? Because small teams don’t hire specialists, they hire people who can figure things out and move fast. 3. I’d create a portfolio. No time for CV polishing. I’d open Canva or Notion and design a simple page showing: ~ Skills I can offer ~ Tools I’ve used (even free versions count) ~ A Loom video introducing myself (authenticity sells) ~ One or two small sample projects—even if I did them myself. 4. I’d search Twitter and Slack channels everyday. I’d type “remote job,” “hiring,” “freelance help” in search bars every morning and evening. I’d join Slack groups for remote workers, freelancers, and async startups (most of them post direct jobs not listed anywhere else). 5. I’d cold pitch 10 people per day with this message: “Hey [Name], I saw you’re building [Product]. I’m a generalist from Nigeria who can help with [X]. I took a look at your [site/Twitter], and I’d love to support you part-time while you scale. I’ve attached a quick Loom showing what I can offer. I can start today, even if it’s just a trial.” 6. I’d offer value BEFORE asking for pay. Even if it’s 2 days of free work. Why? Because when you’re broke, trust is your currency. You need a way in. If they like your work, payment will follow, plus a testimonial you can flip into the next gig. 7. I’d stop overthinking titles. Whether it’s customer support, virtual assistant, community manager, content researcher, all na remote work. Start with where you are. Your goal is to break in, not to look perfect. 8. Once I land one gig, I’d document everything. I’d turn the process into proof: “Here’s how I landed a remote job in 2 weeks from Nigeria with zero connections.” That post alone can attract recruiters or clients from all over. It’s not luck. It’s about packaging, persistence, and proximity to people who are hiring. If you’re broke and serious about remote work, stop scrolling. Start hunting like your next meal depends on it.
