Big shoutout to Tom Pestridge for the killer carousel 🔥 1. Framing → How you present something matters more than you think. The same message can feel totally different depending on the words you use. 2. Affordability Framing → Break down big numbers into smaller, manageable chunks. It feels way more doable. 3. The Rule of Three → When people have three options, they usually skip the cheapest one. 4. The IKEA Effect → If someone puts effort into something, they’ll value it more—just like that shelf they built themselves. 5. The Power of Free → Add the word “free” and watch interest shoot up. People love getting something for nothing—even if they don’t need it. 6. Contrast Pricing → Want something to look like a bargain? Put it next to a more expensive option. 7. Choice Overload → Too many options = decision fatigue. Fewer, clearer choices lead to more action. 8. Anchoring → The first price you mention sets the tone. Everything after that gets compared to it. 9. Endowment Effect → People value things more when they feel like they already own them—so get them imagining what it’s like to use your product. Just having a good product isn’t enough. You’ve got to guide people toward choosing it. The best marketing? It’s part science, part psychology— And all about knowing what drives people to buy.
