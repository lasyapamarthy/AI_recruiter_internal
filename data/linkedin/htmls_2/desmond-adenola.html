But not in the way you’d expect. A few years ago, I stumbled upon the term "sustainable fashion" while trying to figure out how to market a thrift shop I wanted to start. That shop never happened, but sustainable fashion stayed with me. Since then, it’s grown to mean more than just a marketing buzzword. It now means caring for my clothes, the environment, and the people behind what I wear. ‘The environment’ was something I’d always been conscious of. Growing up, my mum worked for the Ministry of Environment, and piles of handbooks on climate change and plastic waste were commonplace around the house. I’d pick them up, read a little, and leave overwhelmed. The world felt too big, and my actions felt too small to matter. That helpless feeling stayed with me for a long time. Years later, that feeling stayed with me as I began researching fashion waste and its impact. The fashion industry generates 92 million tonnes of textile waste every year and is responsible for up to 10% of global carbon emissions. These emissions contribute directly to global warming. However, when we talk about fashion, we rarely talk about this. So I decided to stop feeling helpless. I started asking the important questions: What can I do? What can we do? There were tons of resources and guidelines, but few spoke to my context as someone living in Nigeria. I wanted something that felt real, relevant, and rooted in the African experience. That’s why I’m building Threadit Threadit is a sustainable fashion media brand—a space to start conversations, share solutions, and explore what sustainable fashion can look like for us. I believe fashion should not only feel good, but do good. If you feel the same, I'd love for you to be part of the journey.
