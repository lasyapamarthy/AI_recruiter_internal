As we are starting to realize how good the model is, this is triggering a profound change not only in our view of the AI ecosystem, but it also has triggered a reset of the discussion around the industry structure and regulatory environment. Below my personal thoughts (not speaking for anyone). I would love to hear what all of you think. LLaMA 2 is good. Very good. Meta’s technical report suggested it to be about as good as GPT-3.5. Alpaca Eval now ranks it above GPT-3.5 and Claude 2. The exact ranking may depend on the use case, but the reality is we now have a viable open source GPT-3.5-turbo competitor. And that changes a lot of things. First, it allows an ecosystem of open LLM’s to evolve. We have seen from the Stable Diffusion community how this can cause a Cambrian explosion of creativity and tools. We can expect great, fine-tuned models for specific use cases, rare languages, and niche communities. Privacy-sensitive CIOs and consumers can elect to run the model behind their firewall or on their laptops instead of relying on OpenAI. Or you can host it on Replicate. The important thing is that you have a choice. Second, it renders much of the industry structure debate moot. Should we be worried about a few large companies controlling our AI future? With open models, the answer is "No, we don't!". Closed source seems to be ahead of open source by only ten months. AI has been democratized; you have the freedom to innovate, and that’s a very good thing. Third, aggressive regulation like the EU AI Act seem increasingly disconnected from reality. Any high school student can now fine-tune and run a top-tier model on their laptop and build a so-called “high-risk” app. Requiring a conformity assessment before he or she uploads it to GitHub is not going to work. To be clear, GPT-4 today is still ahead of any open source LLM. But even it increasingly seems within reach. Combining a number of specially trained (or fine-tuned) Llama 2-size models in a Mixture-of-Expert architecture might get us there fairly quickly. In the end, it seems what really matters for LLMs is the training data. The Renaissance democratized the spread of knowledge and put everyone on equal footing.
