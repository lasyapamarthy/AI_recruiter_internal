Yet, text chunks often provide imprecise context, adding noise that can reduce accuracy. Knowledge graphs have been identified as the next evolution to improve the reliability of AI agents. What is a Knowledge Graphs❓ A knowledge graph is a data model that links entities via relationships with each other. An example could be: "Paris is the capital of France", where "Paris" and "France" are entities, and "is the capital of" is the relationship connecting them. Simply put, knowledge graphs put data in context. How did the Knowledge Graph trend begin❓ From my perspective two key forces enabled the shift to integrate knowledge graphs with RAG applications: ▪️ LangChain’s support for Neo4j, one of the leading graph database platforms. ▪️ A study by Juan Sequeda which showed up to 54% accuracy improvements when using knowledge graphs to supply context to LLMs. So what is the difference between Knowledge Graphs and Vector Databases❓ Vector databases store information as high-dimensional vectors to find content that is similar in meaning to a user’s query. However, they often use larger chunks of text, which can include a lot of irrelevant information. This extra noise can make results less accurate. Knowledge graphs, on the other hand, work by identifying key entities (like names, places, or locations) in a query. They then use these entities to find specific, related facts. This structured approach leads to more accurate and context-aware answers. So why isn’t everyone using knowledge graphs❓ They are hard to build. Most knowledge graphs use tools like RDF and OWL which are mostly used in academia and biomedicine and are not very common elsewhere. Right now, most RAG apps use vector databases not because they work better, but because they are easier to use. 🔑 Key Takeaways: ▪️ Knowledge graphs provide more accurate and structured context for AI. ▪️ Adoption is growing with tools like LangChain and studies show up to 54% accuracy gains. ▪️ Still not mainstream because they are complex and harder to build than vector databases. 🗣️ Favorite Statement: “… accuracy increases to 54% when questions are posed over a Knowledge Graph …” 📎 Links: Sequeda’s study: https://lnkd.in/drVYgAW4 LangChain Advanced RAG: https://lnkd.in/dkp_tEem 💬 What’s your take?
