The setup: • Cursor AI (Sonnet 3.7) • Lovable for creatives • Next.js + TypeScript + Tailwind • Vercel for deployment My rules: 1 No writing code myself 2 AI should handle all dev work and content writing, end-to-end 3 I set the idea, theme, and direction — AI executes 4 SEO, deployment, config, and boilerplate — AI’s responsibility 5 No generic output — everything follows my creative lead The reality check: • ⏳I didn’t write a single line of code — but I did spend most of my time reviewing, correcting, and re-aligning the AI’s output. • 🧐Context drift was a real challenge. Unless I kept feeding reminders, the AI veered off into building features I never asked for. • 🖼️Designs? Underwhelming. I had to step in with clear layout decisions, and don’t get me started on SVGs — dozens of iterations and prompts and still not up to the mark. • ✨ SEO was the highlight. Once properly prompted, AI delivered complete metadata, schema, sitemaps — everything wired up cleanly. • ⚙️ CI/CD and project scaffolding were flawless. The initial setup was ready in minutes — smooth and accurate. What I learned: 🚫 AI is not building production-ready apps solo — yet. You're still doing dev work, just in a different format. Think PM + TL + QA — all rolled into one. Oversight is everything. But, it does speed up the work, at least 10x. ⚡ For experienced devs, AI is a serious accelerator. It reduces friction on repetitive tasks and increases output velocity — but you have to know what good looks like. ⚠️ For junior devs — proceed with caution. Use AI as a mentor, not a shortcut. Don’t skip the fundamentals. Understand why the generated code works (or doesn’t). Otherwise, you're trading short-term speed for long-term stagnation. 🛠️ Debugging with AI is shockingly effective. When prompted right, it’s like having a senior dev pair with you — pointing out issues, offering clean fixes, and explaining the why behind it. Conclusion: AI is a phenomenal assistant — but it’s not replacing developers. Not now, and not anytime soon. What it is doing is transforming how we build. It's no longer about whether to adopt AI in your workflow — it's about how well you do it. The idea that PMs, designers, or non-devs can now "just use AI" to build apps end-to-end. While AI does lower the barrier to entry, engineering still requires engineering thinking. You need context awareness, debugging skills, system understanding, and a strong eye for what’s technically feasible — all of which come with experience. AI changes how we build, not who builds.
