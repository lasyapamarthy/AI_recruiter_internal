After almost 7 years in tech and growing into a Senior Engineer at Microsoft, I’ve learned this one truth the hard way: Never judge a developer by their green graph alone. Here’s why: 1️⃣ Many developers work in private repos, Bitbucket, internal Git, enterprise tools. Their graph might look “empty” but they’re shipping real code every single day. 2️⃣ Commits can be faked. A few Git commands and suddenly it looks like someone’s been coding non-stop for years. That green streak doesn’t always mean skill. 3️⃣ Students and early-stage developers often showcase everything publicly. Experienced devs are often busy fixing production issues or scaling systems, things that rarely show up on GitHub. 4️⃣ A contribution graph won’t tell you if someone writes maintainable code, debugs complex issues, or mentors juniors. It won’t tell you how they handle 2 AM outages or tricky product trade-offs. What actually matters to me? → Do you solve problems deeply and with clarity? → Can you collaborate well in a team setting? → Do you understand the business behind what you’re building? → Are you curious, reliable, and growing? A green GitHub graph is nice to look at, but it’s not the story I would hire for. If your graph isn’t perfect, don’t worry. Focus on becoming a developer who can build, ship, and solve real problems. That’s what great teams care about.
